04 / Computational thinking

Math behind the screen

This is the model that makes the project more than a page of facts.

STEP 01Collect dataRecord city, fuel price, source and date.
STEP 02OrganiseStore entries in a structured table.
STEP 03CalculateFind averages, differences, percentages and cost.
STEP 04InterpretUse charts and scenarios to explain the result.

Average price

Average = Sum of values ÷ Number of values

Shows the mean of the prices entered—not a national official average.

Price gap

Gap = Petrol price − Diesel price

A positive gap means the petrol value entered is higher.

Percentage change

Change % = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100

Measures the relative increase or decrease when old price is not zero.

Important: city prices can vary by date and source. A small dataset does not prove why prices differ. Record evidence and avoid claiming that one factor caused a difference unless you have supporting research.
05 / Exhibition ready

Viva questions you can explain

Short answers to help you present the project confidently.

1. What problem does your project solve?

It helps users organise fuel-price research and estimate travel fuel costs using mathematics, instead of only reading separate prices.

2. What is the mathematical model?

A set of formulas that converts inputs such as distance, mileage and price into estimates such as litres required and total cost.

3. What is Computational Thinking here?

Collecting data, organising it, applying step-by-step calculations, finding patterns and presenting results visually.

4. Is this live fuel-price data?

No. Users enter the prices they research. Every value should include a source and checked date. Demo values are only for testing.

5. Why can’t Google do the same thing?

Google can find information, but this project lets the user enter a consistent dataset, compare it, run custom what-if scenarios, calculate trip costs and export the data for analysis.

6. Where is the data saved?

In the browser’s local storage on this device. It does not automatically sync to the teacher’s or another student’s device; exported CSV can be shared.

7. What are the limitations?

Results depend on the accuracy and size of the entered dataset. Trip costs are estimates and exclude tolls, traffic, detours and changes in mileage.

8. How can it be improved later?

By adding more verified data, better source tracking, a larger dataset and—if securely configured—a shared online database.
